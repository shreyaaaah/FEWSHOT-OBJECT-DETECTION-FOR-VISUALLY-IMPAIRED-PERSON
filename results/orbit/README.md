## Honest reading
* Learned features beat the colour histogram clearly. **Frozen DINOv2 (B) is the best closed-set model** (81% at K=10): none of our fine-tuned variants (C, C-xv, D) improved closed-set accuracy over it (differences 0 to -5 points; at K>=5 C, C-xv and D are lower).
* Fine-tuning helps **unknown-object rejection**: open-set AUROC (max-cosine) at K=5/10 is 0.76-0.79 for C/C-xv vs 0.73/0.72 for frozen DINOv2; C-xv (trained with cross-video episodes) is the best at K=5 and 10 (0.790 / 0.780).
  Cross-video training did not change closed-set accuracy (62.4 vs 61.1 at K=1, 75.5 vs 76.5 at K=5: inside the confidence intervals).
* LoRA (D) was lower than partial fine-tuning (C) on closed-set accuracy (73.3 vs 76.5 at K=5); its AUROC is about the same as C (differences inside the confidence intervals).
* Fine-tuning cost vs benefit in one sentence: it buys better rejection of objects the user has NOT enrolled, not better identification of enrolled ones.

## Evaluation bug found and fixed (important)
In every `episodic_*` log above, the row **"ours" (calibrated memory) shows a correct-ID rate of exactly 0.0 +- 0.0. That number is INVALID.**
`src/eval/episodic.py` compared the memory's lower-cased object name with the raw ORBIT name (`P189__keys`), so a correct identification could never match.
Synthetic tests passed because their names were lower case. Fixed in the code (`norm_name`), with a regression test (`tests/test_episodic_names.py`);
the "ours" rows must be regenerated by re-running the evaluation (not yet done). The "closed-set accuracy", "mean-proto@t" rows and the AUROC columns do not depend on this comparison.
The FAR of "ours" (acceptance of unknown objects) was computed correctly.

## What the memory does, measured on the VALIDATION split (diagnosis, 30 episodes, read-only; `logs/pipeline_log_diag_*`)
Fraction of genuine queries whose score reaches the object's own calibrated threshold (note: this is acceptance only, not yet the identity/margin test) and false-accept rate on unknown objects,
compared with the best single global cosine threshold that keeps FAR <= 5%:

| model | K | calibrated memory (TPR / FAR) | best single global threshold with FAR<=5% (TPR / FAR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| B frozen DINOv2 | 1 / 5 / 10 | 25.9/0.0, 44.3/1.4, 53.1/2.9 | 47.9/4.8, 37.1/4.8, 41.2/4.7 |
| C | 1 / 5 / 10 | 36.3/0.4, 58.1/2.8, 61.9/4.7 | 54.8/4.7, 51.3/4.7, 42.8/4.7 |
| C-xv | 1 / 5 / 10 | 39.8/0.6, 59.1/3.3, 64.6/5.5 | 53.1/4.9, 48.6/4.9, 36.9/4.9 |

At K=5 and K=10 the per-object calibrated thresholds accept more genuine queries than the best single global threshold at similar or lower FAR (about +10 and +20 points TPR);
at K=1 they are more conservative (a single enrolment frame gives little to calibrate with). This supports the calibrated-memory idea at K>=5 but is validation-split, acceptance-only evidence;
the corrected test-split "ours" rows are still to be produced.
